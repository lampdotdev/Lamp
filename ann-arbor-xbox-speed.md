Make the game run smoothly on the Xbox Series X: 60 frames a second in one view and at least 30 in split screen, around the Diag and Kerrytown as well as on Main St. It runs at about 30 there now and under 20 split. The console's processor is the limit, not its graphics chip or the screen size: every draw costs time to set up, and the game makes about 650–990 of them a frame. Keep the Mac's version as it is; change only what the Xbox tier gets (device-tier.js and what reads it).

1. Sun shadows: these are about 40% of each frame on the Xbox, since the two cascades draw the whole scene twice more. Turn real-time shadows off on the Xbox and put a cheap stand-in under things so it doesn't look flat: a soft dark blob under each car, person, tree, bench and piece of street furniture (one instanced mesh for all of them), and a darker band where buildings meet the ground. If that looks too plain, try instead one shadow map that's drawn again only when the player has moved about 10 m (`shadow.autoUpdate = false`, `needsUpdate` when they've moved), and keep whichever looks better at 60.
2. Split screen: no real-time shadows when it's on, whatever the tier, since each half draws everything again. When split screen turns on, the browser also rejects draws ("Active draw buffers with missing fragment shader outputs"): find the pass that draws into two buffers with a material that writes one, and fix it.
3. Trees: one material over the leaf atlas the trees already use, so each level of detail in a cell is one draw, and trees cast no shadows.
4. Landmarks: share materials where they're the same (the stadium field's 245 planes and its small boxes each have their own), split the big campus-wide meshes per building, and on the Xbox don't draw a landmark or a whole far part (the airport, the stadium from campus) more than about 1 km away. The camera's far plane is 60 km; on the Xbox bring it in to what fog already hides.
5. Draw at 1920×1080 on the Xbox (scale 1 in its tier) instead of 1440×810 stretched up: the frame isn't limited by its size, and the stretch is why it looks soft there.
6. Arriving somewhere new stutters for about 10 seconds: on the Xbox, warm the shaders of what's near before the player gets there, and spread loading a cell's things over several frames.

Do them in that order and check the frame rate after each one: the `[frame]` line the game logs every ten seconds says how many draws and how long the render takes. Lamp now gives you what the game logged on the Xbox when you look, so read the Xbox's frame line, not the Mac's. Note what you changed and the frame rate before and after in APP.md.
