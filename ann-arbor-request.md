Make Ann Arbor feel real and alive. Work down this list in order, putting each one into the game as it's done, and keep APP.md's Data section and credits.json up to date (credit the City of Ann Arbor for its data, which states no license).

1. Trees by species and size: use the street tree inventory's COMMONGENUS/COMMONNAME, DBH and HEIGHT (.lamp/reference/aa/aa_trees.geojson) so maples, oaks, honeylocusts and the rest look like themselves at their real sizes.
2. Real day and night: compute Ann Arbor's sunrise and sunset locally (no network), and light the streets at night with the city's streetlights (ckan.a2gov.org dataset "streetlights", GeoJSON).
3. Named places: fetch OpenStreetMap's shops and amenities (nodes and ways with shop=*, amenity=*, tourism=* and their names) and show signs on real cafes, restaurants and shops downtown; show the city's building names (Bldg_Name) as you pass, and add the best ones as teleport spots.
4. Campus and civic styling: use the building data's Owned_By (University, City, Parks, Schools) and Struc_Type so U-M buildings get campus limestone and brick, schools and city buildings look the part, and commercial buildings downtown get storefronts.
5. TheRide buses: real routes and stops from TheRide's GTFS (https://www.theride.org/sites/default/files/google/google_transit.zip), driving on schedule; live positions from https://rt.theride.org/gtfsrt/vehicles if that's simple. Don't use TheRide's logo or livery (that needs their permission): plain city-bus colors.
6. Traffic rules: speed limits, lanes and one-ways from OpenStreetMap (maxspeed, lanes, oneway) and the city's road centerlines (https://a2maps.a2gov.org/a2arcgis/rest/services/RoadsSpeedlimitCrash/MapServer/1, outSR=4326), so traffic drives the right way at the right speed and main roads are busier.
7. Crosswalks: zebra stripes at OpenStreetMap's crossings (crossing, crossing:markings).
8. Today's weather: Ann Arbor's current conditions from the National Weather Service (https://api.weather.gov/gridpoints/DTX/42,30/forecast/hourly, with a User-Agent header), so it rains, snows or is foggy when Ann Arbor is. Fall back to fair weather offline.
9. Roofs and floors: OpenStreetMap's roof:shape, building:levels and roof:levels where they exist, for gabled and pitched roofs and true floor counts.
10. Real addresses: house numbers (OpenStreetMap addr:housenumber, or the city's address points) on doors, and taxi fares to real addresses.
11. Street details: fire hydrants (ckan.a2gov.org "fire-hydrants") at the curb, and park amenities (ckan.a2gov.org "park-amenities"): benches, shelters, courts.
12. The Huron River: its flow from the USGS gauge (https://waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/iv/?sites=04174500&format=json&parameterCd=00060) makes it run higher and faster after rain.
13. Amtrak: Wolverine trains stopping at the Depot Street station on Amtrak's schedule (https://content.amtrak.com/content/gtfs/GTFS.zip).
14. Street parking where it really is: OpenStreetMap's street parking tags on roads (parking:both, parking:left and parking:right, with :orientation for parallel or angled, and :fee for metered) say which sides of a street have parking, about 190 streets mapped, most of them downtown. Put parked cars and, where there's a fee, parking meters along those curbs, and keep the other streets' curbs clear. The city publishes no map of its meters (the downtown's parking operator only lists garages and lots), so where OSM has no tag, put meters only on downtown commercial streets (Main, Liberty, Washington, State, South University) and none in neighborhoods.
15. Land use and historic districts: the city's land use, zoning and historic districts (ckan.a2gov.org "land-use", "zoning-districts", "historic-districts") so neighborhoods look like what they are, with older facades in historic districts.

Keep the game fast: build what can be built ahead into the generated data files, and fetch only the live feeds (weather, river, buses) while playing.
