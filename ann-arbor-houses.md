The houses look bad: every house is a box from its footprint's bounding box with the same painted walls, so the neighborhoods look generated. Make them look like Ann Arbor's real houses (Old West Side, Burns Park, Water Hill: 1900s–1920s two-storey wood houses, bungalows and foursquares, some brick, deep porches, gables facing the street, mature trees). Work down this list, putting each into the game as it's done, and look at a few neighborhoods in daylight before and after.

1. Real shapes: build each house from its true footprint polygon (the city's building footprints), not its bounding box, so L-shapes, wings, bump-outs and back additions show. Split a footprint into a main block and smaller wings with their own, lower roofs.
2. Roofs that fit: a main gable facing the street on narrow lots, hipped roofs on square foursquares, cross-gables on L-shapes; dormers on 1.5-storey houses; OpenStreetMap's roof:shape where it's tagged. Visible eaves with overhangs and fascia, not a roof sitting flush on the walls.
3. Variety: siding colors from a real Ann Arbor palette (whites, creams, sage and olive greens, grays, muted blues, barn red, yellow), trim in white or a contrasting color, about one in five houses brick (red or brown), a few stone or stucco. Vary window sizes and placement, door color, shutters on some. Seed it from each house's id, so a house always looks the same.
4. Porches and details: front porches with posts and railings on most older houses, steps up to the door, porch lights on at night, chimneys on the roof's side, foundations visible above the ground.
5. Lots: a front walk from the sidewalk to the door, the driveways and garages the game already has, a lawn, a shrub or two by the porch, and the street trees already in place.
6. Ready-made models, only if they fit the look: Lamp's free asset library has Kenney's "Suburban Houses" and "City Kit" (CC0). Try them on a block and use them only if they match the game's style. If they look like toys next to the rest of the city, improve the generated houses instead. Credit anything used in credits.json.

Keep it fast: one mesh per area as now, details that only show up close drawn only up close, and no drop in frames.
