# The theme follows the subject

Much of a look is often made of what the app is about: a hot sauce shelf's pink wall, chilies and pepper ratings; a
campfire songbook's taped cards, embers and lantern art; an Italian coach's Neapolitan terracotta.

When a request changes what an app is about (hot sauce to wine, campfire guitar to jazz piano, a wedding in Naples to
a trip to Kyoto, a pet to a cactus), whoever made the app, change that look with it in the same request, unasked:
palette, backgrounds and motifs, the style its pictures are drawn in and the pictures themselves, lettering, the
symbols it rates or counts with, its words and their tone, its example content, and the icon.

- Choose the new palette and the icon's new subject and tint before anything else and build with them, but save
  icon.json at the end, with the other changes that need the app reopened, and paint the new pictures right after
  it: they take its colors.
- Make it as particular to the new subject as the old look was to the old one, never plainer: a wine cellar, not a
  generic shelf.
- Keep the kind and its structure, whatever still fits (a wooden shelf holds records as well as books), and whatever
  the person chose or styled themselves.
- An app whose look owes nothing to its subject (system colors and SF Symbols) changes only its words and symbols,
  and a change within the same subject (a wishlist of sauces, a new screen) keeps the look.
- Replace only example content, never the person's own items, and keep saved data readable (keep raw values, map old
  ones to new).
- It changes much of what they see, so work in passes.
- Update APP.md's `Look:` line to the new look.
